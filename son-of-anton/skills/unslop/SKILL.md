# Unslop

Make the writing easier to understand without changing its meaning, evidence,
or intended tone.

- Lead with the point. Remove ceremonial openings, flattery, repeated summaries,
  and generic closing offers.
- Use familiar words and concrete verbs. Replace abstract promises with what
  the thing does. Preserve exact technical names and necessary terminology.
- Cut filler and forced contrasts. Keep qualifications that express real
  uncertainty, conditions, compatibility, or risk.
- Name the actor and the source when known. Remove unsupported attributions;
  do not invent facts, quotes, citations, or measurements to sound specific.
- Give each sentence one clear job. Vary sentence length naturally. Keep the
  articles and connecting words needed to read it once.
- Use prose for connected ideas and lists for sequences or comparisons. Remove
  decorative formatting and repeated bold labels that add no information.

Read the result for lost meaning. Prefer a clear original sentence over a
rewrite made only to satisfy a style rule. Preserve quotations and code unless
the task explicitly includes editing them.

Return the edited text. Explain a change only when it affects meaning or the
user asks for commentary.
